# What this is not

*The limits on everything in this repository, collected in one place. Each one is also stated where the result it bounds is reported; this page exists so a reader does not have to assemble them.*

---

## The hard limits

**Nothing here is a prediction a measurement could contradict.** No quantity in this repository has been compared with an observation of the world. The one result that touches an established framework compares ED with **another model** — causal dynamical triangulations — and a disagreement between two models is a failed correspondence, not a refutation of either against nature.

**There is no inertia, no momentum, no metric and no field equation.** A pattern in these models moves while something is biasing its next commitment and **stops when that stops**. So:

- what is measured is always a **rate of drift, never an acceleration**;
- **nothing here can produce an orbit or a free fall** in the mechanical sense;
- and that is a property of the rules, **not a stage the work has yet to reach.**

**Space is an input.** Which places may be neighbours is given to every model. ED's P06 states the dimension; it does not build the adjacency. **Model result 16 is the finding that space cannot be made from ED's other ingredients**, so this is known rather than overlooked.

**Nothing organised itself.** Every knot and every loop in this repository is seeded. No structure, and no dimension, arose from the medium.

**Matter's persistence is a rule.** A matter pattern's internal links are reserved for their own partners, so it cannot stop committing. **The clock well is a consequence of that rule**, not evidence for it.

**And the rule is more permissive than the ontology's own requirement, which matters for how deep the well is.** Event Density's position is that if commitment stops, a pattern does not persist. In these models a reserved slot renews only when **both** of its places hold possibility, and at the published density it **fails to do so in 91.8% of its attempts and holds a commitment only 31% of the time** — yet the pattern stays, because a slot that cannot pay simply keeps trying. **Applying the requirement instead, with the ontology's own timescale — a slot unpaid for longer than a commitment lasts, ħ ticks, is given up — matter dissolves and the well goes with it.** At the published density **99% of its pairs are gone within 500 ticks and all of them by 3,000, leaving a well of 0.4% where the figures above read 34–36%.** Matter persists only in a richer medium, and there the well is small for the saturation reason above: at **four** units a place **42%** of pairs survive with a **3.1%** well and are still eroding at the end of the run, and at **eight** units all survive with a **1.4%** well. **Across every density tried there is none at which this matter both persists under that requirement and slows nearby clocks by more than a few per cent.** **The figures in this repository are measured with the permissive rule**, and what fails under the strict one is the reserved-slot construction of matter rather than anything in the ontology — **what could hold a pattern together without a reserved slot is not addressed anywhere here.**

**Nothing has been read by anyone outside.** No one who works on causal dynamical triangulations, on discrete gravity, or on complex systems has reviewed any part of this.

## Known negatives, kept rather than dropped

**Nothing falls, and this was looked for.** The clock well is a gradient in rates, not a force. Asked directly whether a free pattern moves down it, the answer is **no** — an unpinned pair is found **less** often beside matter than beside an inert obstacle of the same shape. **The shortage that slows a clock also pushes patterns away.**

**Matter does draw matter together, but only under a rule we added.** Above a coupling we chose, loops gather and the heavier pair settles at a standoff. **The steering weight that produces this is ours, including its strength** — see `INPUTS.md` §6. Without it, loops are pushed apart.

**A bigger mass does not reach further.** Across matter of radius 2 to 5 the clock well's skirt does not widen. **What sets the reach is the medium, not the mass** — which is not how gravity behaves, and is unexplained.

**Reach and strength cannot both be had.** A medium whose clocks notice a shortage is one that refills it quickly. This is recorded as the hardest open item in `results/Constraints.md`.

**The clock well is not shown to be unscreened**, and above the noise floor it has not been read beyond about three shells.

**The polarity phase has no variable in any model here.** P09's U(1) polarity, and P11's randomisation of it, describe a quantity nothing in these models carries. **So nothing here speaks to either.**

**The medium is uniform, not structured.** It holds one coherent domain across nearly the whole box; it does not differentiate into regions.

**One memory setting, one engine per tier.** Results are not cross-checked between the continuous-amount models and the event-layer engine except where stated.

## The shortage's shape, reproduced — and one claim whose conditions matter

**The shape of the shortage has been reproduced on a second source.** Result 25 reports that in a rich medium the shortage around matter matches the lattice Poisson solution for a drain of that size in a box of that size, with nothing fitted; that was measured around the 123-place knot at four units of possibility per place. **It has since been measured around a single free loop of 24 in a box of 30 at eight units per place, and matches the same unscreened solution to 0.7%** with one amplitude fitted — while a screened fit is off by 15 to 21%. **So the unscreened shape is a property of the shortage and not of that one source.**

**But the density matters, and the clock well is a different quantity.** At **two** units per place the shortage is mildly screened (best fit about six places, against 2.6% for no screening at all), and **the rate deficit — the clock well itself — is genuinely screened at about two places at that density.** **So the shortage reaches and the clock's response to it does not.** The reason is that a place's blink rate is a saturating function of the possibility available to it: in a rich medium nothing is near the threshold and the rate barely responds (a 1% well at eight units per place against 17% at two), while the shortage keeps the same shape. **The reach-against-strength trade below is that saturation.**

**And the well is weak at the other extreme too, for the opposite reason, so it has a middle band where it is strongest.** Thinning the medium below one unit a place weakens it again: measured at average densities 1, 0.5, 0.25 and 0.1, the first shell inside the matter reads **36, 27, 18 and 1.6% slow**, and the shells further out reach background by a tenth density. **The cause is that matter's spending is not a fixed amount.** A matter place renews its reserved commitment only when both ends of it hold possibility, so in a lean medium matter cannot pay either: renewals fall from **3.35 a tick to 0.07** across that ten-fold thinning, and the share of renewal attempts that fail for want of possibility rises from **91.8% to 99.9%**. **Matter stops starving its neighbours because it has stopped eating.** So across density the well runs about **2% at a tenth, 36% at one, 35% at two, 1% at eight** — **strong in a middle band, weak above it because clocks stop responding, weak below it because matter stops spending**, and the published figures sit in that band. *(A further thing this measures, and it is worth knowing: at the published density matter's reserved slot is active only **31%** of the time and **91.8%** of its renewal attempts fail. The rule is that it renews the moment it ends if both places can pay, and that is what the model does — but a matter place holds its internal commitment far less of the time than the phrase suggests.)*

**The figure of merit.** The repository counts 38 declared ingredients and judges itself by domains over free parameters with a fixed denominator. **The denominator did not stay fixed:** five mechanisms were added at the event layer, and one of them — the agreement pull — is not grounded in any primitive. **A measure whose denominator moves is weaker than it looks**, and `INPUTS.md` §6 lists what moved.

## What the repository does claim

That ED is **runnable** — its statements were turned into exact rules and run; that it **forbids** things from its own content, including one- and two-dimensional worlds and handedness from mirror-symmetric transport; that some of its own concepts **behave as claimed** when built, notably that matter going without slows the clocks around it and that a head start in becoming cannot be repaid; and that its conservation laws **constrain** a parameter another framework tunes by hand, with the placement in 3+1 measured and negative.

**Each of those is a claim about rules, not about the world.**
