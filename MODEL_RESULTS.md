# What ED's ingredients do: models in supplied space

*2026-09-24 to 26. A companion to [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md). The working record — every model, ledger entry, withdrawal, script and data file — is held separately and available on request.*

---

## What this is, and what it is not

Early attempts asked ED to **make** space. That was a known long shot, since ED's ontology does not claim to make space (three-plus-one is a declared input), and it did not succeed ([Negative_Results.md](Negative_Results.md)). What those attempts learned shaped the model used here, in which **space is supplied as a 3D grid, and the question is what ED's own ingredients do in it.**

The ingredients, and nothing else:

- becoming flows from concentrated toward diffuse;
- committed becoming slows the clocks around it;
- becoming commits above a threshold, irreversibly at that moment;
- committed becoming dissolves after a fixed time, **ħ** — read here, following the author, as *how much memory a pattern can carry before it dissolves*, one fixed number for the whole substrate;
- new places keep being born (expansion);
- commitments use up a budget;
- participation carries a **polarity**, a phase (primitive P09), transported with the stuff (P05).

**These are models, not derivations.** Nothing below derives gravity, particles or cosmology, and nothing is a measurement of nature. Each result is scoped to the rules, sizes and settings run.

**How it was run.** The observables were fixed before each model, not the success criteria. Every instrument was calibrated on cases with known answers, including a null. Expected behaviors were written down before each run. Every claim that failed is kept on record with its reason, and about twenty were withdrawn or narrowed along the way. Two working sessions checked each other's results throughout.

## The results

**1. Structure has a switch, and the switch has a formula.** Lasting structure forms when *interaction strength × commit rate × ħ × threshold* exceeds **1**. This was derived on paper, and then confirmed at the predicted value at two very different ħ, with nothing tuned. A spreading drop needs more (about 1.6–2.1), because clumping has to outrun the spreading. *Strength: derived and confirmed.*

**2. The only memory is the present state.** Remove a clump's committed stuff and it re-forms on the spot. Spread its free stuff away and it never returns. Move that stuff and the clump follows it. Nothing stores where a clump was. This agrees with the ontology's *"nothing accumulates a record of itself"*. *Strength: direct test, three seeds.*

**3. Structure is temporary, ended from outside or from inside.** Without an ending, clumps sustain themselves indefinitely. Expansion (new places being born) thins them until they cannot rebuild. Spending (commitments using up a budget) makes structure burn its own budget. **Either way the whole arc appears: uniform, then structure, then uniform again**, as the ontology's §12 describes. *Strength: observed, all runs; both endings.*

**4. A law for when structure dies under thinning.** For weakly interacting patterns, a formula derived from the flow rule, with five measured inputs and nothing fitted, predicts lifetimes to within about 4% on the runs it was built from, and **still holds at an expansion rate it was never built on** — 0.89 and 1.02, so within about 11% out of sample. For strongly interacting patterns, clumps shelter each other and die together; that case is bracketed but not solved. *Strength: a law, tested out of sample, for weak interaction.*

**5. ħ shelters structure, more the harder it is driven.** Against thinning, the effect is absent right at the switch and grows steadily above it (2.6× at the strongest setting). Under spending, a longer ħ lengthens structure's life, roughly as a square root, though measured tightly that is only a first approximation. *Strength: empirical and reproducible; not derived.*

**6. Grain size and exclusion.** Made of discrete grains, the ink keeps the same switch, and the switch **falls steadily as grains get denser — past the smooth-ink value rather than settling on it** (about 3–4 at the sparsest setting, 1–1.5 in the middle, below 0.75 at the densest). Grain noise holds a weak instability back when grains are sparse; where it ends up depends on how the smooth threshold was translated into grain counts, which was never fixed. With **at most one grain per place, structure never forms at all**, because the amplifier works by piling up and one-per-place forbids the pile. *Strength: observed; the grain model's exact limit depends on a translation choice.*

**7. Patterns of different strength.** A strong pattern **hosts** weak ones: a weak pattern that dies almost at once on its own lives 20–30 times longer inside a strong pattern's clumps, even when it is too weak to form structure alone. Two strong patterns **merge** and each outlives its lone self. *Strength: observed, with lone controls.*

**8. Polarity: bind or part, and selection by surroundings.** With participation as a complex amplitude, √bandwidth × e^{i·polarity} (P09), **patterns in step bind even more strongly, and patterns in opposite phase cancel each other's slowing where they meet, separate, and both die early.** At a quarter turn the result reduces exactly to simple addition, which serves as an internal consistency check. A further test asked whether polarity is *selected*. Patterns do not winnow each other, since they stay where they form. But **each pattern's fate is set by its phase relative to the stuff around it:** within about a quarter turn of its surroundings it keeps its structure, and beyond about half a turn it cannot (16 of 17 drops). In scrambled surroundings, no mixed-phase layout keeps any. This matches the ontology's reading of polarity as relational, "chain-in-environment". *Strength: observed, pre-registered; the surroundings' phase was set by hand.*

**9. The medium's phase is inherited from the start — but only if committing preserves it.** Structure condensing out of a uniform field with one phase everywhere (the ontology's One Being) is fully aligned, and runs the whole arc from uniform to structure and back. Phases never change in these rules, and the measured alignment held at its starting value to three digits throughout, including under expansion and spending: a scattered start stayed scattered. Its structure is weaker and shorter-lived (about a third shorter at one radian of scatter), and a scrambled start forms structure only briefly (peaking at about a tenth of the becoming) with none of it lasting. *Strength: observed and pre-registered; two seeds agree to within 0.004 on alignment and exactly on lifetimes. The constancy is measured, not proved: phase-dependent clumping could in principle favour one phase, and did not measurably.*

**Tested since, and the conclusion changes.** Every model above leaves out something the ontology's own commitment primitive asks for: P11 has a chain's participation collapse to a single channel *with its phase randomized uniformly* — commitment is ED's decoherence mechanism. With that switched on, **no beginning holds structure on its own**, and a single-phase start fails exactly as a scrambled one does, to the digit. Structure comes back only when commitments are required to **agree in phase with a common local target**, and it then lasts about half as long as the best case without randomization (9,000 steps against 18,000) — from any start, so the start stops mattering. A filter blind to phase, rejecting exactly as often, does nothing at all. But **the target's origin makes no difference**: the medium's own phase, a fixed constant, and a frozen random pattern all perform alike. So what structure needs is commitments that **agree in phase locally**, and these models do not show what they agree with — the ontology's tension-polarity rule is one target that works, and is not distinguishable from two that carry no ontological weight. **Tighter agreement does not buy more, though.** Narrowing the window raises how closely commitments agree — 0.63, then 0.90, then 0.97 — without lengthening anything: the lifetime holds at 9,000 and then falls to 6,000 once seven commitments in eight are being turned away, with the peak falling throughout (0.53, 0.49, 0.31). Agreement is bought by rejecting the commitments that do not fit, so it costs quantity, and structure needs enough commitments as well as agreeing ones — while a phase-blind control at that same low acceptance still dies at 2,000, so the agreement is what does the work. **The unrandomized case is the one where agreement and quantity hold at once, and P11 is what takes it away.** **And the flow contributes nothing positive.** The local flow entered the filter as a sign: commitments aimed at the target where stuff was gathering and at the opposite phase where it was dispersing. Switching that sign off makes structure last **longer**, not shorter — 9,000 steps against 5,000–6,000, with a higher peak (0.53 against 0.38) — identically for all three targets and both starts. The reason fits the rest: the sign gives gathering and dispersing regions opposite targets, so commitments disagree across the boundary between them, and local disagreement is the one thing structure cannot tolerate. **So the rescue is local phase agreement alone.** *Strength: observed, pre-registered, with a phase-blind control whose rejection rate matches by construction; every run agrees on lifetime at the 1,000-step sampling resolution and on peak to within 0.004, and the flow's sign shortens structure at both acceptance widths it was compared at. Scope: one grid size; three acceptance widths; and this is for the reading of “the phase of the flow” used here, which the ontology leaves unsettled.*

**10. After the start, a medium can order itself — and one rate decides whether it does.** Under P11, with the commitment filter of result 9 in place, **neighbouring regions come into phase over time**: agreement between adjacent regions rises from about 0.7 to between 0.93 and 0.99 over a run, on both seeds. **This is produced, not supplied.** The filter is never told what to order toward — result 9 showed a fixed constant and a frozen random pattern work exactly as well as the medium's own phase — so the common phase the regions settle into is not one that was handed in. It is the first thing in this programme that makes a medium *more* ordered than it started.

**And it is the whole medium, not only its regions.** From a scrambled start, essentially all committed stuff comes to share **one** phase: global alignment climbs steadily and reaches, by about 4,000–5,000 steps, within 1–2% of full alignment given the spread each commitment carries. It matters *when* that happens, because the same measure has misled before: it arrives while **a fifth to a quarter of all the becoming is still committed**, not as the last few grains wink out. The contrast is visible in the same runs — with the strong tie to the local clock, alignment falls all through the run and only jumps at the very end, when almost nothing is left, which is the artefact and is read as one.

**But order arrives after structure has passed its peak, not before it.** Structure peaks at step 1,000, when alignment is only 0.16–0.25; alignment reaches its ceiling around step 4,000–5,000, when committed stuff has fallen to about a fifth of its peak. **So the medium is most in step when there is least of it.** The ordering does not enable structure — it accompanies structure's decline, and nothing here shows it running the other way.

**What undoes it is ED's own slowing.** Polarity is a phase on a rule's update cycle, and updates run on the local clock, which committed matter slows — so phase advances more slowly inside a clump, like a clock carried up a mountain. Tie polarity's turning weakly to the local clock and the ordering is untouched. Tie it as strongly as 1/ħ and **the ordering inverts**: agreement falls away over the run, on one seed past zero into anti-alignment. Structure's lifetime is unchanged either way (9,000 steps in every case), which is what result 9 already implies — structure does not use the global phase pattern. So **the models put an upper bound on a number the ontology does not fix: the tie between polarity's turning and the local clock must be well below 1/ħ, or ED cannot order its own medium.** That rate is deliberately kept separate from ħ rather than set equal to it: ħ here is a lifetime, this is a rate, and identifying them would make a pattern's phase drift over its own lifetime equal to the tolerance it needs to survive — which is to choose the point where the two effects collide.

Route dependence is real but small for anything passing through: two probes leaving one place and arriving at another by different routes, one through a clump and one around it, differ by about a hundredth of a turn — one clump-site's worth of slowing — with a same-route control reading exactly zero. The large effect is on what *stays* in a slowed place, not on what crosses it. *Strength: observed, pre-registered, two seeds agreeing on direction and magnitude; the no-advance and weak-advance cases are separated from the strong one by the whole range of the measure. Scope: one grid size, one switch strength, one acceptance width, and the free medium's own alignment remains unreadable under the rule set before these runs — the ordering is measured on committed stuff.*

**11. Where a new event goes barely matters — once space is given.** ED does not say where new becoming appears. Every model above quietly answered it: expansion was applied as the same fractional thinning everywhere, **which is exactly the claim that new places appear evenly everywhere** — a choice carried silently by eleven models. Four readings were compared, each drawn from the ontology's own text: evenly everywhere (the silent default); where becoming is densest; where clocks run fastest, away from committed matter; and **at commitments**, which has the strongest textual claim of the four — *“every micro-event enters the universe through a commitment event”*. Each was run so that **exactly the same total becoming is removed per step** and only its location differs, matched to one part in 10¹⁵, and each was run with its pattern live and frozen so that place could be separated from feedback.

**The choice costs at most about a fifth of structure's life, and changes nothing else measured.** Placing new space where becoming is densest costs 11–17%; at commitments, 17–23%, the largest of the four; where clocks run fastest, nothing measurable. The full arc from uniform to structure and back survives under every reading, the whole-medium ordering of result 10 still reaches its ceiling under every reading, and clump counts move by about 5%. Feedback — the pattern responding to the structure it shapes — accounts for about 6% at most. **So in models where space is supplied, ED does not need to answer “where” in order to work.** That is the question every one of the fourteen earlier attempts ran into, and here it turns out not to bear on anything measured.

**Two things this does not show.** It does not climb the wall those attempts hit: they asked whether new events *build* space, which remains closed, and this asks only where new capacity appears within space that is handed in. **It walks around that question rather than answering it.** And *“evenly everywhere” and “where clocks run fastest” cannot be told apart here* — but that is a blind spot of these models, not an equivalence: free becoming and slowed clocks barely overlap, because free and committed becoming are separate quantities and the free kind sits where nothing has committed. A model in which they overlap could distinguish them; these cannot, and so they say nothing either way about whether dense regions fall behind as space is added elsewhere.

**One reading was checked against another of ED's own statements.** “New events enter at commitments” and “commitments use up a budget” (result 6) are mechanisms of the same shape, so they might have been one statement twice. With spending switched off in both, the at-commitments rule still shortens structure — 10,000 steps against 13,000, and 35,000 against 44,000 — so they are two different statements, not one. *Strength: observed, pre-registered, two seeds agreeing exactly on lifetimes. **No reading is ruled in or out**, and none could be: a reading that leaves structure alive longer is not thereby the true one, and ruling one out would need it to make ED's world impossible, which none does. Scope: one grid size, two base models, one expansion rate; the at-commitments rule matches the others' total only to within a few per cent, since concentrating dilution on the few places committing at a given moment occasionally asks for more than is there.*

## Where the models touch established physics

These are resemblances, not claims.

- Matter gathering where clocks run slow is how ED describes gravity, and it is what the models do.
- Settled structures surviving while the loose material between them thins resembles bound galaxies in an expanding universe.
- Complexity rising and then falling, only when the parts interact, matches Aaronson, Carroll & Ouellette (arXiv:1405.6903).
- The clumping mechanism has a known cousin, motility-induced phase separation (Cates & Tailleur 2015).
- Opposite-polarity patterns cancelling each other's structure resembles matter meeting antimatter, which ED's concept files identify with opposite polarity. The becoming here is conserved throughout; what is destroyed is the patterns' ability to hold together, not the stuff itself.

## What the models could not do

- **Make space.** Five further rules for growing extent from nothing were tried and closed:
  - three counting rules turned out to favour clumps;
  - a memory rule produced closeness instead of extent;
  - a "present-only" rule produced a tangle that mimics three dimensions on one measure (see the caveat below).

- **Choose polarity from the flow.** A polarity that turns with the flow of becoming adds up the same along every route, so it cannot produce phases that depend on history. In these models becoming always flows downhill, which never circulates, so any polarity rule read off the flow has this limit. A second, independent attempt fared no better: letting the flow decide which phases may commit (result 9) makes structure die sooner than ignoring the flow altogether.
- **Push patterns apart without polarity.** Slowing belongs to the place and only ever adds, so everything with structure gathers.

**An instrument caveat worth keeping.** How distance grows with size cannot, on its own, tell three-dimensional space from a critical tangle: both read about one-third. It must always be read alongside a shape measure.

## Open questions for the ontology

1. **What sets the phase of the surroundings?** In models where commitment preserves phase, the start does: alignment is inherited and held. With P11's phase randomization, no start is enough, and structure needs commitments to agree in phase locally; the models do not show what sets that agreement. **Something can:** under P11 the commitment filter brings neighbouring regions into phase after the start (result 10), so the start is not the last word. The whole medium comes into step too, not only its regions, within a few thousand steps — though after structure's peak rather than before it. What remains open is whether ordering can ever *precede* structure rather than accompany its decline, and the one rate that decides whether ordering happens at all, below. Turning phases toward neighbouring ones does not do it.
2. **Is the polarity connection (P05) a field of its own**, or is it read off something else? Partly answered: its *time* component carries real curvature sourced by ED's own slowing, with no new field needed (result 10). Whether it also has a life of its own in space, with its own dynamics, is untested.
3. **How strongly does polarity's turning depend on the local clock?** The ontology does not fix it, and the models bound it: well below 1/ħ, or a medium cannot order itself (result 10). Setting it equal to 1/ħ remains available, as a decision rather than a consequence.
4. **The exact laws** for how long structure lasts under spending and under strong interaction. Both are approximate.
