# What ED's ingredients do: the ink-drop models (B-3 to B-12)

*Attempt B, 2026-09-25 to 26. A plain-language companion to [MODEL_RESULTS.md](MODEL_RESULTS.md); the working record is held separately and available on request.*

---

## The one-paragraph version

Space was taken as given, as the ontology declares, and a drop of ink was put into it with **only ED's own ingredients**: becoming flows from dense to thin; committed becoming slows the clocks around it; becoming commits above a threshold; committed becoming dissolves after ħ ("how much memory a pattern can carry before it dissolves"); new places keep being born; and commitments use up a budget. **The result is the author's ink picture, in full: smooth, then structure, then smooth again. It happens two independent ways: by expansion, and by spending.** Each ingredient has a distinct, measurable job, and the main behaviours were worked out on paper before they were run.

---

## What each ingredient does

| ingredient | what it does in the models |
|---|---|
| **committed matter slows clocks** | **makes structure.** Becoming lingers where clocks are slow, piles up, commits, and slows the clocks further. This loop is the amplifier. Without it the drop only blurs. |
| **ħ (memory before dissolving)** | **sets when structure can form and how long it lasts.** It enters the switch for structure, and it **shelters** structure: under spending, and against thinning **once structure is driven well above the switch**. The sheltering grows with how hard structure is driven: none right at the switch, and 2.6× at the strongest setting tried. |
| **new places being born (expansion)** | **ends the middle from outside.** It thins the free becoming until clumps can no longer rebuild themselves, and everything returns to smooth. |
| **commitments use up a budget** | **ends the middle from inside.** A little of each commitment is spent for good. Structure churns commitments, so it burns its own budget and dies, with no expansion needed. Over half of everything ends up spent. |
| **the present state (no stored record)** | **is the only memory.** A clump's location is carried by the pile of free becoming at that spot. Remove the pile and the clump is gone; move the pile and the clump follows. |
| **graininess** (ink as individual grains) | **delays structure but keeps it.** The same switch and the same runaway clumping; sparse grains need a stronger push. |
| **exclusion** (at most one grain per place) | **forbids structure.** The amplifier works by piling up; one grain per place forbids the pile, so nothing forms at any strength tried. |

## The results, with their strength

**1. Structure has a switch, and the switch has a formula.** Lasting structure appears when *interaction × commit speed × ħ × threshold* exceeds **1**. This was derived on paper, and then confirmed exactly: ripples in a dense region fade below 1 and grow above it, at two very different ħ. **Nothing was tuned.** A spreading drop needs a little more (about 1.6, or 2.1 for long ħ), because clumping has to outrun the spreading.

**2. The only memory is the present state.** This was tested by knocking clumps out, spreading their contents away, and moving them. It fits the author's reading exactly: memory is a trace the present carries, not a record kept somewhere.

**3. Expansion gives the full ink story: smooth → clumps → smooth.** Faster expansion ends the middle sooner. The amount of thinning a clump survives settles to a fixed value when expansion is slow, which is reached clearly for short ħ.

**4. ħ shelters structure, because settled matter doesn't thin.** When committed matter is made to thin as well, the protection almost disappears. The sheltering depends on how hard structure is driven: absent right at the switch, and growing steadily above it (2.6× at the strongest setting).

**5. A law for when clumps die, the strongest single result.** A formula built from the flow rule, with nothing fitted and five measured inputs, predicts clump lifetimes to within about 4% on the runs it was built from. **It was then applied at an expansion rate it was never built on, and still landed — 0.89 and 1.02 against the truth, so within about 11% out of sample.** That is a law, not just a description of one run. It holds for weak interaction. For strong interaction the clumps shelter *each other*, holding the space between them up, so no clump dies alone. That case is boxed in between two formulas but not yet solved.

**6. The return trip happens from inside, too (B-5).** If each commitment spends a little of the budget, structure uses itself up and the ink returns to perfectly smooth, with no expansion. Longer memory and slower spending both lengthen the middle, less than proportionally: roughly as the square root at first sight. Measured tightly (seeds agreeing to 1%), the square root turns out to be a **good first approximation, not an exact law**. The true dependence is a little steeper, partly because slower spending lets more be spent before structure gives up.

**7. Graininess delays the switch; exclusion removes it.** Made of grains, the ink still has B-3's switch and clumping, and the switch falls as the grains get denser. Made of grains that can't share a place, it never clumps.

**8. The two endings together (B-6).** Structure can be ended from outside (expansion) or from inside (spending). With long memory the two act independently and their rates simply add; with short memory they reinforce each other, ending structure up to 29% sooner than either alone would.

## More than one kind of pattern (B-7 to B-12)

*From here on ħ is one fixed number for the whole substrate (the author's decision); patterns differ in how strongly they interact.*

**B-7: strong patterns host weak ones.** A weak pattern that dies almost at once on its own lives 20–30 times longer next to a strong one, sitting entirely inside the strong pattern's clumps. That holds even for a pattern too weak to form any structure alone. The host is not harmed. Slowed clocks belong to the place, so a strong pattern's slowing shelters everything nearby.

**B-8: strong patterns don't compete, they merge.** Two strong patterns end up in the same clumps and each lives longer than it would alone, more so the stronger the partner. This is built into the rules: slowing belongs to the place, everyone adds to it, and it only ever makes things linger. So everything with structure gathers together. Nothing in those rules could push patterns apart.

**B-9: polarity gives patterns a way to part.** Taken straight from the primitives, participation is a complex amplitude, √bandwidth × e^{i·polarity} (P09, Paper 001), and polarity travels with the stuff (P05). With that, **in-step patterns bind even more strongly, and opposite-phase patterns cancel each other's slowing where they meet, separate, and both die early.** It resembles matter meeting antimatter; it is noted, not claimed. In these runs, though, the phases were set by hand.

**B-10: can the flow choose the phases? Not in these models.** Letting polarity turn with the change in event density (the author's reading of the tension-polarity concept) turned out to add up the same along every route, so it can never produce a meeting phase that depends on the journey. Pushing it harder only scrambled each pattern's own phase. More generally, becoming here always flows downhill, and downhill flow never circulates, so any polarity rule read off the flow has this limit. A genuine "phase that depends on the path" would need the connection (P05) to be a field of its own. That has not been built, by choice.

**B-11: is polarity selected? Yes, but against the surroundings.** Patterns don't weed each other out, because clumps stay where they form and rarely meet. **Each pattern's fate is set by how its phase compares with the stuff around it:** within about a quarter turn it keeps its structure, and beyond about half a turn it dies (16 of 17 drops). In scrambled surroundings, no mixed-phase pattern keeps any structure. This is the concept file's own reading, polarity as "chain-in-environment", now seen as a behaviour.

**B-12: the One Being start.** From one uniform field with one phase everywhere, structure condenses, everything that forms is aligned, and the full arc plays out (uniform → structure → uniform, with about half the becoming spent and half thinned away). **Alignment stays at whatever it was at the start**, to three digits, for the whole run. A scattered start gives weaker, shorter structure, and a scrambled start gives only a brief flicker. So in these rules, **a coherent beginning is what makes structure possible at all.** (Measured, not proved: in principle the rules could shift alignment, but here they did not.) **One limit, and it has now been tested — see B-13/B-14 below.**

**B-13 and B-14: can a medium's phase change after the start?** Two attempts. **Turning phases toward neighbouring ones does nothing:** mixing cancels a scrambled medium's phases within a few steps, and turning cannot rebuild what has cancelled. **Then P11 itself was switched on** — the ontology says committing randomizes phase, and no model until now did that. With it, **no start holds structure at all**, the One Being included: a single-phase start fails exactly as a scrambled one. Structure returns only when commitments have to **agree in phase with a common local target**, and then lasts about a third as long, from any start. A filter that rejects just as often but ignores phase does nothing, so it is the phase doing the work. But what the target *is* makes no difference — the medium's own phase, a fixed constant and a frozen random pattern all work alike. So **under P11 a coherent beginning is neither enough nor needed; what structure needs is commitments that agree in phase locally, and the models do not show what they agree with.** Whether the medium itself ever becomes aligned still cannot be read: its coherent amplitude falls away too fast to measure, by a rule set before the runs.

**B-15: a medium that orders itself, and the one rate that undoes it.** Looked at region by region rather than whole, the filter does something new: **neighbouring regions come into phase over a run** (about 0.7 rising to 0.95, both seeds). Nothing told them what to agree on — a constant and a random pattern work just as well as the medium's own phase — so the order is made, not supplied. **And it is the whole medium, not just neighbouring patches:** from a completely scrambled start, nearly all committed stuff comes to share one phase within a few thousand steps, while a fifth to a quarter of everything is still committed — so this is not the “perfectly aligned nothing” that caught an earlier measure. **But the order arrives late:** structure peaks first, and the medium is most in step when there is least of it, so the ordering accompanies structure's decline rather than making it possible. **What undoes it is ED's own slowing.** Polarity is a phase on a rule's cycle, cycles turn on the local clock, and committed matter slows clocks, so phase advances more slowly inside a clump — a clock carried up a mountain. Tie the turning weakly to the clock and the ordering stands; tie it as strongly as 1/ħ and the ordering inverts. Structure lasts the same either way, so **the models set an upper bound on a number the ontology leaves open**: that tie must stay well below 1/ħ, or ED cannot order its own medium.

## Stepping back: what the models say about ED's ingredients

| ED ingredient | what it does in these models |
|---|---|
| committed matter slows clocks | makes structure (the amplifier), and makes everything with structure gather together |
| ħ, one fixed number | sets the bar for structure; shelters structure more the harder it is driven; lengthens structure's life under spending |
| new places being born | ends structure from outside |
| commitments use up a budget | ends structure from inside |
| memory = the present state | the only memory: a clump is wherever its stuff is |
| graininess / one-per-place | graininess delays structure; one-per-place forbids it |
| polarity (a phase) | in step binds, out of step cancels and parts; a pattern survives by matching its surroundings; the start fixes the phase while committing preserves it — once committing randomizes it, what matters is local agreement, not the start |

**Open questions for the ontology**, which the models raised but cannot answer:
1. **What sets the phase of the surroundings?** While committing preserves phase, the start, and nothing later changes it. Once committing randomizes phase, neighbouring regions come into phase by themselves (B-15), so the start is not the last word — and the whole medium comes into step within a few thousand steps, though only after structure has peaked. Whether ordering can ever come *first* is open, and one rate decides whether it happens at all.
2. **Is P05's connection a thing of its own**, with its own life, or is it read off something else?
3. **Where does the translation to grains end up** (B-4's many-grains limit)?
4. **What is the exact law for how long structure lasts** under spending, and under strong interaction? Both are approximate for now.

## Where the models touch established physics (resemblances, not claims)

- **Matter gathering where clocks run slow** is how ED describes gravity, and it is what the models do.
- **Settled structures surviving while the loose stuff between them thins** resembles bound galaxies in an expanding universe.
- **Complexity rising and then falling** matches the published coffee-cup result (Aaronson, Carroll & Ouellette, 2014), including their finding that it needs the parts to interact.
- **The clumping mechanism** has a known cousin in physics: things that slow down where they are crowded clump together (motility-induced phase separation). What is specific to ED is that the slowing comes from committed matter's clocks, and that ħ sets both the switch and the sheltering.

## What was wrong along the way, and was corrected

- The first ink drop "just blurred", because it was built without the amplifier. The card's version had it, and it works.
- A cap on a measurement produced a false "exact" match (1.01). Uncapped, it was not exact, and the claim was withdrawn.
- One correction was itself an overcorrection. A single comparison near the switch seemed to show ħ doesn't shelter, and I narrowed the claim. The other session spotted that it clashed with an earlier row, and a sweep showed both were right at different drive strengths. The overcorrection was withdrawn.
- "Lifetime goes as the square root of memory over spending" looked right at coarse resolution. Measured to 1%, it is only approximate, and it was narrowed.
- Guesses at the mechanism were tested and **ruled out**: a halo around clumps, the last-to-die clump, and nucleation.
- The first grain run had grains moving at mismatched speeds, and a drop that froze in place. Both were fixed before anything was read.
- "The grain switch converges on exactly 1" read too much into the data. The switch keeps falling past 1, and where it ends depends on a translation choice. It was narrowed.

Every one of these is kept on record with its reason, and each correction made a result stronger or more precise.
