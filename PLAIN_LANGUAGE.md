# Event Density in plain language

*Allen Proxmire, 2026-09-27, updated 2026-10-06. The whole programme — what ED is, what testing established, what its ingredients do, and how strongly each result stands — without the technical detail. The full statements are in [results/RESULTS.md](results/RESULTS.md), [results/MODEL_RESULTS.md](results/MODEL_RESULTS.md) and [results/EVENT_LAYER.md](results/EVENT_LAYER.md); how it was done is in [method/HOW_IT_WAS_DONE.md](method/HOW_IT_WAS_DONE.md).*

## The story in one page

**Where it started.** Event Density is an ontology: the world is made of *becoming* before it is made of things, and time only runs one way. The earliest phase of work compared the principles of ED against known physics. That refined the ontology, and more importantly it pointed toward ways to research it. The question became: **what does ED actually establish, when it is tested carefully?**

**Phase 1 — analysis and proof.** Fourteen model builds, each with its meanings fixed in advance and its expected results written down before it ran, inspired in part by Causal Dynamical Triangulations (CDT). Four solid results came out of those builds, and a fifth from later analysis (Part 1). They also confirmed that ED does what its founding statement says: it supplies *"the conditions of possibility, not the full catalogue of outcomes"* — it says what may happen, not where a new event goes, and it takes three-plus-one dimensions as a declared input rather than making them.

**Phase 2 — models.** So the question turned: **take space as given, as the ontology declares, and ask what ED's own ingredients do in it.** Build a model, measure what it does, interpret last. That produced a coherent little world (Part 2) — and, at the end, two checks on space itself: whether it can be grown (it can't) and whether it can be kept orderly as it grows (only locally).

**Phase 3 — one layer down.** Those models all ran on **amounts**: how much commitment is at a place, how fast it drains, a clock read off that amount. **But ED doesn't say the world is made of amounts.** It says becoming happens in separate commitments, each one holding a place for a while and then dissolving back into possibility, and that a clock at a place counts its own commitments. **Amounts are the smoothed-out picture — what the ontology itself calls the shadow.** So the question turned again: **model the events themselves, and see whether the same ontology still works when nothing is averaged.** It does, and it does more than before (Part 3): the medium holds itself together, the arrow of time comes out as written, and matter digs a well in the clocks around it.

## Part 1 — results from analysis and proof

*None of these is new mathematics. Each applies known results to ED's own principles; ED's contribution is the connection — asking the question, and what the answer then fixes or rules out.*

1. **Three numbers CDT tunes are fixed by the ontology.** CDT, a well-developed approach to quantum spacetime, has three numbers it tunes by hand. **ED's conserved budgets fix all three**, with nothing tuned. In 2+1 dimensions the point they fix sits where space holds together. **In 3+1 it has been measured, and it doesn't.** ED needs a vertex density of 0.044; CDT's own value where space behaves itself is about 0.075, and never below 0.073 anywhere looked. Nobody had published that number, so a working CDT program was run to get it. **So the fixing itself still stands — exact, nothing tuned, in any dimension — and the point it picks out isn't where CDT's good phase is.** *This compares two frameworks: it says where ED's point sits on CDT's map, not which of them is right about the world. CDT is itself an unconfirmed approach, so a placement that misses is a failed correspondence between two pictures rather than a measurement against nature — and there are three ways it could miss: the dictionary between them is ours, ED may be a different discretisation altogether, or CDT's own semiclassical phase may not be the physical one. This is the central tested result, and what it tests is a correspondence.*
2. **Below three dimensions, clocks can't stay in step.** In one and two dimensions, the effort needed to keep a pattern's clocks together grows without limit as the pattern grows; from three up, it stays nearly level. It reads the dimension, not the number of connections, and it matches established physics of coupled clocks. So ED's own content rules out one- and two-dimensional worlds.
3. **Handedness can't be written into mirror-symmetric rules.** A proved theorem, with a script that checks it. If the world has a handedness, its state picked it, not its laws — and handedness is possible at all only because time runs one way.
4. **ED carries the dimension it is given.** Measured with ED's own definition of dimension, the rules carry a dimension they are given and add none of their own: the declared input is genuinely an input. With result 2 below and result 5, it is bounded to three, conditionally.
5. **If a particle is a knot that can't be cut, three dimensions is the only number that works.** Two of the ontology's settled meanings do the work: a particle is a knot of commitments that cannot be cut, and that includes its strands not being able to pass through each other. Then **in two dimensions a loop can't be knotted at all**, so there would be only one kind of thing. **In three there are endlessly many knots and none can be undone** without cutting or passing a strand through — so kinds are many and they last. **In four or more, every knot comes undone** by slipping strands past each other through the extra room, so no kind lasts. Put beside the earlier finding that clocks can't keep time together below three, that pins the number from both sides: too few and clocks fail, too many and particles can't stay what they are. The mathematics is old and standard; what the ontology adds is the link — saying a particle *is* an uncuttable knot is what makes a fact about knots into a fact about what can exist. **The 'if' matters:** the argument holds for loops, and two-dimensional sheets *can* knot in four dimensions, so if lasting things could be sheets rather than loops, four reopens. And it still doesn't grow space — space stays an input. It gives a reason for the number. **And models have since shown what makes such a knot uncuttable in ED.** Nothing stays committed, so a particle's loop isn't a set of links that last — it's a pattern of links that keep being re-made, and while any one of them is off the loop is briefly open. **What holds it together is that an empty link's place is kept for its own pair**, by identities fixed when those places first came into being. With that, the knot keeps its kind at every rate tried; without it, it comes apart quickly — a strand slips through a gap, or something else takes the empty place and opens the loop. The reservation is a reading of the ontology's own rules, and what the models show is that it is needed, not that it appears by itself. **And it protects a knot on the move, not just at rest.** A trefoil sent travelling through grown, blinking space **kept its kind in every reading of every run**; without the rule that strands can't pass through each other it came undone in all of them, one ending fully untied. Carrying the knot costs a little ground — about 80 to 95% of what a plain loop of the same length covers.

Also: patterns hold together only above about five connections per event; ED's substrate must be a random web, not a regular grid (a grid would make gravity depend on direction); and in the 2+1 runs ED never collapsed.

## Part 2 — results from models: what ED's ingredients do

**The ingredients, and nothing else:**
- **possibility** — what hasn't committed yet — flows from dense to thin;
- committed becoming slows the clocks around it;
- possibility commits above a threshold, and that commitment is **becoming** — becoming *is* commitment, the tick;
- committed becoming dissolves back into possibility after **ħ** — *how much memory a pattern can carry before it dissolves*, one fixed number for the whole substrate;
- new places keep being born;
- commitments use up a budget;
- every pattern carries a polarity, a phase.

And, in the author's reading, **the rate of commitment is the clock**: committing and dissolving tick at the local clock rate, while new places arrive on one shared beat.

These are models, not derivations: they show what the ingredients do, not what nature does.

1. **Structure has a switch.** Nothing forms until the ingredients are strong enough; then lasting clumps appear. The point where that happens was worked out on paper first (*interaction × commit rate × ħ × threshold* = **1**) and then found exactly there, with nothing tuned — and it stays exactly there whether or not committing runs on the local clock. It is the clearest case of ED's ingredients behaving in a way that can be worked out in advance.
2. **Structure is temporary: the ink-drop picture, in full.** Left alone, clumps keep themselves going. They end in one of two ways, both ED's own: **from outside**, when new places being born thin them out, or **from inside**, when commitments use up their budget. Either way the whole arc appears — uniform, then structure, then uniform again — the ontology's picture of every structure as a temporary attractor.
3. **The only memory is the present state.** A clump is wherever its stuff is. Remove the stuff and the clump is gone; move it and the clump follows. Nothing stores where it was — *nothing accumulates a record of itself.*
4. **How long structure lasts can be worked out from the rules — and for crowded patterns, accurately.** Two formulas, both derived with nothing fitted, for how long structure survives after its peak. A lone clump leaks away on its own, and that case comes out to within about a factor of two. **When many clumps hold the medium up between them, it comes out to within about 15%** — from a single measured quantity, and on cases the formula had never seen. The picture behind it: the clumps keep the possibility around them far above what thinning alone would leave, then give it up slowly, like a reservoir drained to hold a level steady. That crowded case is the one the first formula could only put bounds on.
5. **Patterns affect each other.** A strong pattern **hosts** weak ones: a weak pattern that would die almost at once lives 20–30 times longer inside a strong one's clumps. Two strong patterns **merge**, and each outlives its lone self.
6. **Polarity decides whether patterns bind or part.** In step, patterns **bind** more strongly. Out of step, they **cancel each other and part**, destroying each other's structure — a resemblance to matter meeting antimatter, noted and not claimed. A pattern keeps its structure only if its phase is close to that of the stuff around it: polarity is **selected by the surroundings**.
7. **What structure needs from polarity is local agreement.** Structure condensing out of a uniform, single-phase beginning (the One Being) is fully aligned. And when commitment scrambles phase, as the ontology's commitment primitive says it does, structure forms and lasts when commitments **agree in phase locally** — from any start.
8. **A medium orders itself.** With local agreement at commitment, neighbouring regions come into phase over time, and then the whole medium does: from a completely scrambled start, it comes to share one phase within a few thousand steps. Nothing told it which phase to choose; the order is made, not supplied. It arrives as structure declines rather than before it. **A pattern's phase changes only when something commits** — it does not turn between ticks — and that is what lets this happen: the models show that a phase turning steadily on its own, anywhere near once per memory lifetime, would stop the medium coming into step.
9. **Where new places are born costs little.** ED doesn't say where a new event goes. Four readings from the ontology's own text — evenly, where things are densest, where clocks run fastest, and at commitments — change structure's life by at most about a fifth, and nothing else measured. A clump feels *how much* new space arrives, not *where*.
10. **When commitment is the clock.** With committing and dissolving on the local clock: the switch stays exactly where it was; dense places run slow, so clumps grow and merge about four times more slowly; structure is ended mainly **from outside**, by new places arriving, since dense places now use up their budget only slowly; and dense places fall slightly behind when new space is born elsewhere — always in that direction, by a few per cent.

11. **Order is made by things happening, not waiting in advance.** Possibility in these worlds does come into step — but committing gets there first, and the order possibility has is handed back from settled things as they dissolve. Order supplied at the start is pulled *down* to the same level within a hundred steps, so commitment sets how far in step the medium is, whatever it began with. Letting possibility flow more easily between places already in step — the other thing that might have made order come first — made no difference at all.

12. **Aligned commitments pull their surroundings into line, further the longer they last.** A patch of commitments sharing one phase, set down in a scrambled world, leans both the possibility and the new commitments around it toward its own phase — fading smoothly with distance and still measurable thirteen sites away. That is a field in the author's own sense: not a separate ingredient, but the bias a stable pattern puts on what happens near it. **Its reach belongs to the surroundings, not to the patch:** make the patch twenty-seven times bigger and the reach changes by about a quarter. And it **widens with time**, because the phase is passed along — tracking how far possibility has had time to spread, with short memory capping how far it gets. It needs its source, though: nothing outlasted the patch.

13. **How far a mass's pull reaches.** A mass's load spreads outward and thins over the sphere, and clocks slow wherever it gets to. The load is a **trace** of settled matter rather than a settled thing itself, so it does not dissolve — and it therefore falls off as **1/r, with no cutoff**, which is gravity's form: clocks slow as 1/r and the pull goes as 1/r². **That form is what spreading in three dimensions gives**, so it shows the picture holds together rather than explaining gravity. Two side findings: had the load dissolved, its reach would have stopped at the very same distance that limits the polarity field, and a heavier mass would have reached only a little further. And graininess alone does not stop the reach — load too small to pass on piles up until it tips over, and the front moves on.

14. **Space has to be put in, and that's now been checked rather than assumed.** ED declares that its substrate is three-dimensional. Nine models asked whether the rest of ED could grow that space anyway, starting from events alone with no space supplied. **None did.** They grew long strands, shapeless tangles, or small worlds where everything sits a few steps from everything else. Requiring new events to be born next to each other came closest — routes rejoined and the world stayed even — but it never got the thing space has, **consistent directions**, where going east then north lands you where north then east does. **Why that counts in ED's favour:** the ontology has a rule that an ingredient only earns its place if it does something the others can't. Contrast was once tested that way and failed. Space has now been tested the same way and passed. Declaring the number of dimensions is normal — relativity doesn't derive four either — but ED has now checked that its declaration is doing real work. **And a later round found what the declaration is actually adding.** The ontology already says there is a neighbour relation and that it looks the same everywhere; what declaring three dimensions adds is **that relation's dimension.** Every attempt to grow space has to settle *which* places are neighbours from the structure built so far, which is circular — the structure decides who may link and linking decides the structure — and that is why they either run away into a small world or set into knots. Four ways of settling it from ED's other ingredients were tried and each gave at most half of what space needs, because a rule that gets both halves right is already very nearly a space. **And asking it the other way round gives the sharpest answer.** Instead of looking for something that supplies the neighbour relation, the last test asked whether **possibility** — what hasn't committed — can hold one by itself, dissolving and re-forming under ED's own rules with nothing read from positions or from what has committed. **It heals the bottlenecks** — a maze's 898 single-link places drop to nearly none. **But every start collapses into a world where almost everything is within nine steps of everything — including a start that really was three-dimensional.** So it isn't that possibility can't make a dimension; **it can't keep one it's given.** The reason: banning triangles means a re-formed link can't join places two steps apart, so the closest it can join is three — and with nothing to say which of those are actually near, every re-formed link is a shortcut, measured in a structure the last round already shortened.

15. **ED can cut a region off two ways — by breaking its links or by stopping its clock — and the clock wins.** Bandwidth collapsing to zero makes a real cut: a region nothing crosses, in either direction, sharp to about one place across and sized by the mass. That is the “decoupling surface” the ontology asks for, and it exists **while the region is fed**. **It cannot hold itself up:** seal a region and it starves — nothing arrives, so nothing commits, so the drain that cut the links stops, and the links heal back open. Letting the mass grow doesn't save the seal, it dissolves it, because a heavier mass slows the very commitment that was doing the cutting. **What grows without limit is the slowness.** The clock inside runs hundreds of times slower than outside and keeps slowing, with no floor — but gently, as a power of time, so nothing ever freezes. Such a region gets ever closer to being cut off and never gets there.

16. **ED's ingredients make a glass, not a crystal.** Space can't be made from ED's other ingredients (item 14 above), so the next question is whether it can be **kept** — whether an existing piece of space stays orderly as new places join it, which is what the picture of expansion needs. **Half of that works.** A rule read from ED's own contrast principle — *no triangles, and no two short loops sharing more than one edge* — grows material with the right local arrangement **even from a seed with no order in it**, which is the first time this programme has *made* geometric structure instead of inheriting it. **The other half doesn't.** The order is right on average and wrong in detail everywhere: about a quarter of places are exactly right close up, and almost none a little further out. That combination has a name — it's a **glass.** Window glass has the same local arrangement of atoms as quartz and none of its long-range pattern, and this is the same thing in a network of relations. Eleven attempts to crystallise it all failed, including letting things dissolve and re-form, and melting and slowly cooling. The reason is worth having: **counting loops per link doesn't pin down the arrangement** — many structures satisfy the count and the cubic one is only one of them, and they're separated by barriers so high that anything hot enough to rearrange is hot enough to destroy the local order. And across distance the glass isn't three-dimensional: it's compact and the same in every direction, but a random walk gets trapped in it, as in a maze of bottlenecks, so up close it looks like 3D space and from far away it doesn't behave like it.

17. **ED's rules don't spoil space they're given, and it can be built up one place at a time — what wrecks it is letting a new place join only where it closes a square.** Two things had always changed together in these models: **when** a place arrives, and **what it's allowed to link to** when it does. Separating them settles it. **When** makes no difference at all — outward shell by shell, at a ragged edge, or filling in from inside, all three give one open region that reads three-dimensional on both measures at 400,000 places, and a particle gets across it. **What it's allowed to link to** decides everything. Under the earlier models' rule — after its first link, a place may only link where it closes a square — every order gives the maze, because **a place that arrives where no square can be closed keeps its one link for ever**, and a place with one link is a door by definition. So the thousands of doors *are* those stranded places. **Allow a link that closes nothing and the space survives being built.** The three-dimensionality itself is still supplied, not produced — what's shown is which rules keep it and which destroy it. **And the ruled rule survives ED's own blinking, while the other falls apart under it.** Let a grown space blink — every link dissolving after its span and having to be won again — and it comes out **healthier**: places end up with more links than they had when growth stopped, because anyone who missed a partner gets another chance each time a link dissolves, and the number of places left on a single link drops from thirty-five to a handful. A particle keeps moving in it the whole time. Under the square-only rule the opposite happens: a dissolved link can only be won back across a square, a strand hasn't got one, so the space bleeds links it can't replace and the particle stops when there's nothing left around it. **That's the decisive case against that rule, and it comes from ED's own dissolving rather than from any measure of shape.**

Smaller findings: ħ shelters structure, more the harder structure is driven, and under spending lengthens its life roughly as a square root. Ink made of grains keeps the same behaviour; allowing at most one grain per place forbids structure entirely, because structure forms by piling up.

## Part 3 — results from the event layer: the same ontology, unaveraged

*Blinks instead of amounts. A place is either committed or it isn't; a clock counts its own blinks; possibility is units that get spent and come back. Everything below is read off one set of rules.*

**The starting point is the One Being** — everything in step, possibility spread evenly, nothing distinguished — which is where ED says to start, not from nothing.

6. **Scarcity doesn't tear it apart.** Places share a budget too small to serve them all at once. It neither freezes into a fixed pattern nor scatters into noise: **it settles, by about three hundred ticks, into one coherent medium and stays there** — blink rates **more even than pure chance would give**, neighbours holding a steady gap of about two blinks, nobody starved, no place stuck busy or stuck idle. Two box sizes agree to three decimal places.
7. **What holds it together is that committing is something two places do *together*.** This was found by taking the candidates away one at a time. **Remove the pull that two places exert on each other when they commit, and the whole thing falls apart.** Remove instead the clever rule about *which* partner to choose, and nothing is lost at all. **So whom a place commits with barely matters — that it commits *with* someone is the entire mechanism.** A commitment with no effect on its partner wouldn't be a relation in the first place.
8. **The arrow of time comes out exactly as the ontology states it.** Pile all the possibility into a ball and leave the rest of space quiet. **Becoming spreads out, the gradient flattens, and within a few hundred ticks the beginning is forgotten** — every rate matches a universe that started uniform, to three decimals. **But one thing never fades: the place where becoming began ends up permanently older, by 13 to 18 blinks.** That is time as ED defines it — accumulated becoming, which can't be repaid. **The rates all even out and the age difference stays.**
9. **There is one budget, and it is possibility.** No second limit on how many neighbours can commit at once. Where possibility is scarce the medium spends it as fast as it arrives; **where it is plentiful the only ceiling left is ħ** — a place can't commit again until its commitment dissolves — and that ceiling is the ontology's own, with no number of ours in it.
10. **Matter slows the clocks around it, and this is the one to look at.** A knot of matter has to keep committing to stay what it is, so it spends possibility its neighbours would have had. **Read raw, with no correction of any kind: clocks run slow where the matter sits, and recover by about half per step outward across six steps until they match the background.** Same sign in every run, every box size, every density. **A place near matter is not being pulled on. It is going without.**
11. **The shortage behind that well has the shape gravity has** — in a rich medium it matches the exact textbook answer for a drain of that size to within a percent. **That shape is not ED's achievement and the document says so plainly:** anything conserved that spreads out in three dimensions falls off that way. **What is ED's is the drain** — that matter eats becoming at all, because it has to keep committing to exist. And one real limit came with it: **a medium whose clocks notice a shortage is also a medium that refills it quickly**, so reach and strength are two sides of one thing and you cannot have both.

> **What Part 3 changes.** Phase 2 showed ED's ingredients make a coherent world when space is handed to them. **Phase 3 shows the same thing without averaging anything** — and two claims the ontology leans on hardest, *gravity is the architecture of gradients* and *time is the accumulation of becoming*, stopped being claims and became readings.

## How strong each result is

Not all results carry the same weight, and they shouldn't be quoted as if they did.

* **Proved or exact:** the handedness theorem; the CDT result in 2+1 (exact algebra); the structure switch at 1, derived and confirmed under both clock rules.
* **Measured laws of the rules:** the clock floor below three dimensions, checked against the obvious objection.
* **Clean, reproduced observations:** the memory result; the full ink arc and both endings; hosting and merging; binding and parting; selection by surroundings; local agreement at commitment; the medium ordering itself; where new places are born; the ending balance when commitment is the clock; exclusion forbidding structure.
* **Tested on cases it had not seen:** the lifetime formula for crowded patterns (within about 15%, inside a stated range — four unseen cases).
* **Estimates and approximate trends:** the lifetime formula for lone clumps (about a factor of two); ħ's sheltering; the square-root lifetime under spending; the grain model's switch.
* **The event-layer results (Part 3):** all six are clean, reproduced observations on one ruleset, agreeing across seeds and box sizes — the medium and the arrow to three decimals, the clock well with the same sign in every run. **Two carry stated limits:** the clock well's *reach* was set by the size of the box until the box was made large enough, and the shortage's Newtonian shape is what diffusion in three dimensions gives, so the result there is the drain and not the shape. Everything is at one memory setting.
* **The later results:** order made by commitment, the polarity field's reach, and the 1/r reach of a mass are clean, reproduced observations; space as a necessary input rests on nine models with their measurements fixed in advance on calibrated instruments; the two ways to cut a region off, and the glass, are observed across many runs and seeds, each with its decisive check (box size and grid size for the surface; unrestricted rearrangement for the glass).

## How it was done

* **The author decided every meaning**, in writing, before models were built: distance, the One Being, memory, ħ, where new places are born, and the rate of commitment as the clock.
* **What would be measured was fixed before each run**, not what would count as success.
* **Every instrument was checked on known answers first**, including a null.
* **Two working sessions reviewed each other** before anything was recorded as a result.
* **The code for model results 1–12 is in the repository**, so each can be rerun; the scripts for the later results are available on request.

The full working record is held separately and available on request.

## Where ED stands

* **What it is:** an ontology, with its inputs stated openly (the Born rule, the area law, 3+1 dimensions, a starting shape).
* **What testing established:** it fixes what another theory tunes (three numbers in CDT); it rules things out (one and two dimensions, four or more if a particle is an uncuttable knot, handedness in symmetric rules, a grid substrate); with space given, its own ingredients produce a coherent world of structure, memory, hosting, binding and parting, self-ordering, and a return to uniformity; space itself is a necessary input; and grown space keeps its local order but becomes a glass. **And modelled as events rather than amounts, the same ingredients hold a medium together under scarcity, deliver the arrow of time with an age that never fades, and put matter at the bottom of a clock well — with no metric and no field equation in the model.**
* **What it leaves open, by design:** where a new event goes — what the commitment that makes a place decides about its relations — and every specific structure. ED supplies the conditions of possibility, not the catalogue.
* **The open questions:**
  1. **Answered.** What sets how high possibility sits around a clump: in a rich medium the shortage around matter takes the textbook shape for a drain, matching it to within a percent, and where the medium is leaner it is screened with a reach of about five steps. What remains open is narrower and sharper — **why a bigger mass does not reach further**, which is not how gravity behaves. (Whether polarity is a field of its own is answered too: nothing in ED is, and committed polarity acts as an emergent field with a reach set by how far possibility spreads in one memory lifetime.)
  2. Exact lifetime laws in the hard cases.
  3. What the commitment that makes a place decides about its relations — the deepest one. (Whether new events build space is answered: they don't, and space is an input.)
